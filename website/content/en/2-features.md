# Features

ZeGrapher draws mathematical objects in the plane — functions, sequences,
parametric equations and data — and exports exactly what you see.

- **Functions** written the way you write them on paper, `f(x) = 2 + cos(x)`.
  You get the usual mathematical functions, plus every object you define
  yourself.
- **Sequences** from their recurrence relation, first terms included:
  `u(n) = 0 ; 1 ; u(n-2) + u(n-1)`.
- **Parametric equations**, drawn over the interval you choose.
- **Constants and variables** shared by every object, so a single value drives a
  whole family of curves. A constant can
    - slide between its two bounds, by hand or as an animation,
    - hold several values at once, and every curve that uses it is then drawn
      once per value.
- **Data** imported from CSV, tested on files of several million cells, and
  edited in a table like a spreadsheet.
- **One world of objects**: any named object can appear in the expression of
  another, and in the cells of a data column too.
- **A graph you can navigate**: drag to move, scroll to zoom, one axis at a time
  if you want.
- **Customization down to the grid**: ticks as multiples of an expression such
  as `π`, and separate subdivisions and colors for x and y.
- **Exports identical to the preview**, vector (`svg`, `pdf`) or image (`png`,
  `jpeg`, `bmp`, `ppm`). You give the size in real centimeters or in pixels.
- **Your work saved** in a document, and reopened at the next start.
